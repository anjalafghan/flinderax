# Couples finance patterns and privacy/security for "joy" (self-hosted Indian couples finance app)

Scope: Part A, how couples manage money together (research and apps). Part B, privacy, security and LLM handling for a self-hosted finance app. Researched November 2026, with about 16 searches and fetches. Many app comparison pages are affiliate or vendor blogs, so their claims are marked as weaker.

## A1. Money models (joint / separate / yours-mine-ours / proportional) and the research on satisfaction and conflict

### Takeaway
The best experimental evidence (JCR 2023) and a large multi-study paper (JPSP 2022) both find that pooling money protects relationship quality. The proposed mechanism is "financial togetherness" and goal alignment, not the account itself, and the authors say autonomy is not harmful in itself. Money is a common topic of conflict that keeps coming back, mostly over fairness and responsibility. Indian survey data (advisory firm, not peer-reviewed) shows frequent money arguments and a preference for hybrid arrangements.

### Cited Findings
- Olson, Rick, Small and Finkel, "Common Cents: Bank Account Structure and Couples' Relationship Dynamics," *Journal of Consumer Research* 50(4):704-721 (2023), DOI 10.1093/jcr/ucad020. It was a six-wave longitudinal experiment with 230 engaged or newlywed couples who had separate accounts. Couples were randomly assigned to open a joint account, keep separate accounts, or get no intervention. — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:jconrs:v:50:y:2023:i:4:p:704-721.); [ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2023/05/230504111937.htm)
- Result: the separate and no-intervention groups showed the normal decline in relationship quality, while the joint group kept quality steady. The joint group was buffered against decline rather than significantly improved. Proposed mechanisms are better feelings about how money is handled, financial goal alignment, and sustained communal norms. — [Kellogg Insight](https://insight.kellogg.northwestern.edu/article/key-to-happy-marriage-joint-bank-account); [Newswise](https://www.newswise.com/articles/married-couples-who-merge-finances-may-be-happier-stay-together-longer)
- Caveats: about 20% of couples dropped out, including a notable share of separate-account couples who split up. The sample was limited to newlyweds. — [Newswise](https://www.newswise.com/articles/married-couples-who-merge-finances-may-be-happier-stay-together-longer)
- Gladstone, Garbinsky and Mogilner, "Pooling Finances and Relationship Satisfaction" (JPSP, 2022; six studies, N = 38,534). Couples who fully pool their money report higher satisfaction and break up less often. The effect is strongest for low-income or financially distressed couples, and "financial togetherness" mediates it. The authors say financial autonomy is not inherently harmful. What matters is seeing possessions and goals as shared. — [UCLA Anderson Review](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/joint-bank-account); [Paper PDF](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Gladstone-Garbinsky-Mogilner_Pooling_Finances_and_Relationship_Satisfaction_2018.pdf); [CU Boulder](https://www.colorado.edu/business/faculty-research/2022/12/10/pooling-finances-and-relationship-satisfaction)
- British Cohort Study data in the same work: by 2010-12, 30% of those who kept all money separate in 2000-02 had separated. The figure was 26% for partial poolers and 24% for full poolers. This makes "yours/mine/ours" (partial pooling) an intermediate case. — [UCLA Anderson Review](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/joint-bank-account)
- Garbinsky and Gladstone (2019, *Journal of Consumer Psychology*): pooled money changes spending, because people feel a greater need to justify purchases to their partner. Joint accounts led to more "wise" or utilitarian spending. — [Notre Dame Mendoza](https://mendoza.nd.edu/?p=7598); [zendy DOI 10.1002/jcpy.1083](https://zendy.io/title/10.1002/jcpy.1083)
- Gottman: a Gottman Institute post lists money among the six most common areas of marital conflict in Gottman's research. — [Gottman blog "M is for Money"](https://www.gottman.com/blog/m-is-for-money/)
- A Gottman-associated figure (secondary source): about 69% of marital problems are "perpetual," meaning they recur. This applies to all problems, not money specifically. — [Business Insider India](https://www.businessinsider.in/A-relationship-therapist-reveals-the-best-way-to-prevent-money-from-ruining-your-marriage/articleshow/55822828.cms)
- Diary research (secondary summary): money is not the most frequent conflict, but money conflicts are harder to resolve, recur more and cause more stress. Peetz et al. found the main themes are fairness and responsibility, plus "mundane expenses." Couples who could discuss small money disagreements reported better relationship quality. — [Nordot summary](https://nordot.app/1139983318921462648); [Psychology Today](https://psychologytoday.com/za/blog/the-teen-doctor/202307/why-do-couples-really-fight-about-money)
- India: a 1 Finance survey of 1,258 people in Tier 1 and Tier 2 cities (reported 2025) found 67% of couples argue about money regularly, some as often as every fortnight. About 46% of women reduced work or quit after marriage, and 56% earned less than their husbands. Among divorcees, 42% of men cited "financial issues" and 43% of women cited "financial disparity." — [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/personal-finance/42-men-took-loans-for-alimony-46-women-reduced-work-after-marriage-survey); [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/personal-finance/story/divorce-costs-42-of-men-took-loans-for-alimony-legal-costs-says-survey-495720-2025-09-25)
- India: a 2023 BankBazaar study (as cited by LoansJagat) found 38% of couples feared misuse of funds in a joint account, which pushes couples toward a hybrid of individual autonomy plus a shared bill pool. — [LoansJagat](https://www.loansjagat.com/news/how-indian-couples-are-redefining-money-management-in-modern-relationships) (secondary; primary BankBazaar report not retrieved)

### Inferences
- For joy, the evidence supports making the shared "ours" layer (the date jar, savings jar and joint goals) the emotional centre, while still allowing private accounts. The research credits shared goals and a sense of togetherness, which the jar framing supplies, not literally merging bank accounts.
- Fights are mostly about fairness and responsibility, so settle-up should show its logic openly: who paid what, the split rule used (50/50 or by income), and a running balance. This turns arguments about fairness into a number both partners can check.
- Pooling makes people feel they must justify spending. A small private "no-questions" allowance per partner can keep that from feeling like surveillance. This is an inference, not a tested claim.
- The Indian data (income gaps, women leaving work) suggests a proportional-to-income split option is relevant. No source above directly tests proportional splitting against 50/50.

### Gaps
- Found no peer-reviewed study comparing proportional-to-income splitting with equal splits on satisfaction.
- Found no primary Gottman study on money specifically. The Gottman "money date" or "state of the union" practice was not sourced in this round.
- Indian evidence is from advisory-firm surveys (1 Finance, BankBazaar), not peer-reviewed research. No academic Indian study of couples' account structures was found.

## A2. What couples apps offer: shared vs private visibility, settle-up, joint goals, bills, check-ins

### Takeaway
The market splits into two kinds of app. Household budgeting apps (Monarch, YNAB Together, Honeydue) give each partner a login over a shared view, with varying control over what stays private. Expense-splitting apps (Splitwise, GPay split) track IOUs and settle-up. Few do both well: YNAB's own guidance is to pair it with Splitwise. Zeta, the couples-specific app, shut down in 2025.

### Cited Findings
- Zeta (joint finance for couples) reportedly shut down on 9 May 2025. Acorns acquired its assets in June 2025 but did not keep the couples app running. — [PocketClear blog](https://pocketclear.app/blog/zeta-app-alternative-couples.html) (vendor blog; not confirmed with Acorns)
- Honeydue: free. Each partner links their own accounts and chooses what to share in a shared view. It has privacy settings to hide accounts or transactions, plus bill reminders and partner notes or chat. — [Origin ranking](https://useorigin.com/resources/blog/ranking-the-best-finance-apps-for-couples); [envelopebudgeting.com](https://envelopebudgeting.com/articles/honeydue-alternatives) (secondary; sources disagree on ads and sync reliability)
- Monarch: one paid household subscription (Core plan, about $99.99/yr per comparisons). Each partner has their own login and sees shared accounts, budgets and goals. Partners can connect their own accounts without merging. — [Origin, partner apps 2026](https://useorigin.com/resources/blog/finance-apps-that-let-you-add-a-partner-in-2026); [Stash](https://www.stash.com/learn/best-budget-app-for-couples/)
- YNAB Together: groups of 2 to 6 people on one subscription, each with a separate login. Members can be given access to specific plans only. — [YNAB announcement](https://www.ynab.com/introducing-ynab-together-for-your-shared-financial-journey/); [YNAB budgeting together](https://www.ynab.com/budgeting-together); [YNAB partner support doc](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/partner-HJqX5Br1j.md)
- YNAB's official guide to using it with Splitwise: create a "Splitting" category, record payments to and from the partner at settle-up, and use a "Settle Up" transaction so purchases made on your behalf land in your own categories. This is evidence that budgeting and splitting are usually separate tools. — [YNAB: Splitwise and YNAB](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/splitwise-and-ynab-a-guide-H1GwOyuCq.md)
- Splitwise: tracks who paid and who owes, and shows exactly what to pay at settle-up. — [YNAB guide](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/splitwise-and-ynab-a-guide-H1GwOyuCq.md)
- Monarch's shared household use is described as creating household "lock-in": once both partners rely on it, switching is harder. — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/chat/h/d8600f0b-b848-475c-ab78-098f71bcf66f/)

### Inferences
- The common feature pattern is per-partner login, per-account visibility toggles (shared, balance-only, or hidden), shared goals or jars, bill reminders, and a settle-up ledger. joy's mix of a date jar with rollover, a savings jar and settle-up closes the budgeting-vs-splitting gap that YNAB fills by pointing users to Splitwise.
- Privacy toggles per account and per transaction (Honeydue's model) are the main way apps respect individual spending privacy.
- "Money dates" or scheduled check-ins fit naturally with joy's date jar, for example a monthly review date that shows jar rollover and settle-up status.

### Gaps
- Did not retrieve official docs for Goodbudget (shared envelopes across devices) or Tricount (now owned by bunq). Their features are not verified here.
- Did not find an official Honeydue privacy or help page. Its visibility details come from comparison blogs.
- No app was found with a built-in "money date" or check-in feature, although some blogs claim one. Not verified.
- No India-specific couples budgeting app with real traction was found. Some small apps appear in search (for example coupl.money), but their usage could not be verified.

## A3. Indian context: joint finances after marriage, wedding costs, family obligations, UPI splits

### Takeaway
Indian weddings average roughly ₹30-40 lakh, depending on the survey. They are funded mostly from savings, with about 15% using loans. Joint-account suspicion and income gaps after marriage are common. UPI apps (Google Pay) have built-in group split since December 2021, but it is basic: equal or custom shares plus payment requests.

### Cited Findings
- WedMeGood's 5th Annual Wedding Report 2025-26 (2,000 couples marrying April 2025 to March 2026, plus 500+ industry professionals) puts the average wedding at about ₹39.5 lakh, up 8% year on year. Destination weddings average about ₹58 lakh, and Jaipur reaches about ₹73 lakh. — [WedMeGood report](https://www.wedmegood.com/blog/wedmegood-annual-wedding-industry-report-2025-2026/amp); [Curly Tales](https://curlytales.com/india/ct-scoop/in-now-destination-weddings-jaipur-leads-indias-wedding-budget-spend-at-lakh-more/amp/); [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/business/2025-lavish-indian-weddings-thrive-with-8-higher-spending-despite-soaring-gold-prices)
- Funding: about 79% rely on savings and about 15% (15.2%) take loans, borrowing ₹15.5 lakh on average. Venues, jewellery, decor and catering are the most loan-funded items. About 6.25% liquidated assets. — [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/banking/loan/big-fat-weddings-cost-couples-their-hard-earned-savings-only-15-per-cent-use-loans-report); [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/insurance/story/your-daughters-big-day-why-wedding-insurance-deserves-a-look-amid-rising-costs-risks-508352-2025-12-27)
- WeddingWire India put the 2024 average at about ₹29.6 lakh, so figures vary by methodology. — [shaadinews](https://shaadinews.com/2025/04/16/how-much-do-indians-spend-on-weddings-survey-reveals-average-costs-that-will-blow-your-mind)
- Jefferies estimated that Indian weddings cost about 3x average annual income. — [Benzinga India](https://in.benzinga.com/content/39503756/indian-weddings-cost-3-times-the-average-annual-income-jefferies-sees-opportunity-for-lenders)
- Google Pay India split expense launched December 2021. You create a group, choose "Split an expense," enter the total, include or exclude members, and send requests, then track who has paid in the group chat. — [Google Pay India Help](https://support.google.com/pay/india/answer/11420982?hl=EN); [Business Today 2021](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/story/google-pay-gets-split-expense-feature-heres-how-it-works-316663-2021-12-23); [XDA](https://www.xda-developers.com/google-pay-india-split-bill/)
- Joint-account fear (38%) and hybrid arrangements — see A1, [LoansJagat](https://www.loansjagat.com/news/how-indian-couples-are-redefining-money-management-in-modern-relationships).

### Inferences
- An Indian couples app should model family obligations explicitly: transfers to parents, sibling education, and wedding contributions from each side. These are a likely source of fairness conflict. Settle-up and the "ours" budget should be able to tag them separately from couple spending.
- joy can generate UPI deep links (`upi://pay?pa=...&am=...`) for settle-up rather than moving money itself. This keeps the app out of payments regulation. That is an inference: the UPI intent spec was not sourced here.

### Gaps
- No rigorous data was found on how wedding costs are split between families or partners, or on how often couples open joint accounts after marriage in India.
- No data was found on Splitwise's user numbers in India. The 20-member limit for GPay split is unverified.
- Family remittance or obligation data (for example, the share of income sent to parents) was not found.

## B1. DPDP Act 2023 and DPDP Rules 2025: applicability to a personal or couple app, and obligations if offered to others

### Takeaway
Section 3(c)(i) excludes "personal data processed by an individual for any personal or domestic purpose." A self-hosted app the developer runs for himself and his partner very likely falls within that exclusion. Offering joy to other couples (as a hosted service) makes the operator a Data Fiduciary. Most duties take effect around 13 May 2027 under the phased rollout of the Rules, which were notified on 13 November 2025. They include notice and consent, Rule 6 security safeguards (encryption, access control, logs kept for a year), breach intimation and processor contracts.

### Cited Findings
- Section 3(c)(i): the Act does not apply to "personal data processed by an individual for any personal or domestic purpose." — [dpdpa.com Section 3](https://dpdpa.com/dpdpa2023/chapter-1/section3.html) (unofficial reproduction; check against the Gazette)
- The personal or domestic exclusion is in Section 3 (outer scope), while Section 17 is a separate set of exemptions. These include national security and a power to exempt notified fiduciaries, such as startups, by volume and nature of processing. — [Mynd DPDP wiki](https://dpdp.myndsolution.com/wiki/guides/who-does-the-act-apply-to/); [Tsaaro](https://tsaaro.com/blogs/regulatory-carve-outs-examining-exemptions-under-the-dpdp-act-2023-and-dpdp-rules-2025); [dpdpa.com s.17](https://www.dpdpa.com/dpdpa2023/chapter-4/section17.html)
- The DPDP Rules 2025 were notified in November 2025 (G.S.R. 846(E), 13 November 2025; some reports say 14 November). Definitions and the Data Protection Board provisions took effect immediately. Consent-manager registration starts November 2026. Core fiduciary duties, data principal rights, penalties and cross-border rules apply from about May 2027 (18 months). — [Tribune India](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/govt-notifies-digital-personal-data-protection-rules-rollout-in-phases-specified-obligations-for-data-fiduciaries); [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2025/11/digital-personal-data-protection-dpdp-rules-2025-rollout-over-18-months); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/data-protection/1708434/operationalising-indias-data-protection-regime-analysing-the-newly-notified-dpdp-rules-2025); [Mynd Rule 6](https://dpdp.myndsolution.com/wiki/rules/rule-6-reasonable-security-safeguards/)
- Sources conflict on the timeline for Significant Data Fiduciaries: one says 24 months, others say 18. Check the Gazette. — [Mondaq phased rollout](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/india/data-protection/1706878/indias-digital-personal-data-protection-act-phased-rollout-and-key-compliance-milestones)
- Rule 6 (reasonable security safeguards) names these measures: encryption, obfuscation, masking or virtual tokens; access control; visibility of data access through logs; measures for continuity and backups; keeping logs and personal data for at least one year for detection and investigation; and contract terms requiring safeguards from Data Processors. — [dpdpa.com Rule 6](https://dpdpa.com/dpdparules/rule6.html); [PwC India](https://www.pwc.in/assets/pdfs/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025.pdf); [KS&K](https://ksandk.com/md/data-protection-and-data-privacy/dpdp-rule-6-and-indias-new-cybersecurity-compliance-standard/); [Thales brief](https://cpl.thalesgroup.com/sites/default/files/content/compliance_brief/india-dpdp-rules-2025-compliance-guide-cb.pdf)
- Breach notification is in Rule 7. The often-quoted 72-hour detailed report to the Board was not confirmed from a primary source in this round. — [Mondaq highlights](https://www.mondaq.com/india/data-protection/1709066/key-highlights-of-the-digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025)

### Inferences
- Personal use (one couple, self-hosted): likely outside DPDP under s.3(c)(i). It is still sensible to follow Rule 6 practices voluntarily, since they match normal security hygiene.
- An edge case is the partner's data. The developer processes his partner's financial data too. Data shared within a household for a domestic purpose plausibly stays "domestic," but there is no authority on this. Explicit consent from the partner within the app is low-cost protection.
- If offered to others: the operator becomes a Data Fiduciary. Duties include an itemised notice and consent in plain language, purpose limitation, Rule 6 safeguards, breach intimation to the Board and affected users, erasure when the purpose ends, grievance redressal, and contracts with processors. LLM API providers would count as Data Processors, and Fly.io hosting outside India raises cross-border questions. Financial data is not a special category under DPDP, but scale could trigger Significant Data Fiduciary status.
- Pulling bank data through India's Account Aggregator framework would add separate RBI and Sahamati obligations. For a personal app, user-uploaded statements and SMS parsing avoid that.

### Gaps
- Exact Rule 7 breach timelines and Rule 8 erasure periods were not verified from the Gazette text.
- No guidance was found on whether a couple's shared app counts as "domestic purpose" when one person runs it for both.
- Section 3 and 17 commencement (some say 13 May 2027) is unverified against the Gazette.

## B2. Security baselines for a self-hosted finance app

### Takeaway
SQLCipher gives strong page-level encryption at rest for SQLite: AES-256-CBC, HMAC-SHA512 per page and PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA512 key derivation. It only helps if the key is kept off the same disk, which on Fly.io means a secret injected at runtime. Combined with encrypted backups, access logs and the encryption, masking and logging items in Rule 6, this gives a defensible baseline.

### Cited Findings
- SQLCipher (v4 design) encrypts each page individually with 256-bit AES-CBC and a random per-page IV. It appends an HMAC-SHA512 over the ciphertext and IV, checked on read, so tampering is detected. The key comes from the passphrase via PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA512 with a per-database salt stored in the first 16 bytes. The HMAC key is derived separately. — [Zetetic SQLCipher design](https://www.zetetic.net/sqlcipher/design/); [SQLite3MultipleCiphers SQLCipher scheme](https://utelle.github.io/SQLite3MultipleCiphers/docs/ciphers/cipher_sqlcipher) (lists kdf_iter default 256000)
- Rust forks differ: the rusqlcipher fork uses HMAC-SHA256 and its `sqlcipher` feature excludes `bundled`. Check which build or feature is used (rusqlite has a `bundled-sqlcipher` feature, not verified here). — [rusqlcipher README](https://docs.rs/crate/rusqlcipher/latest/source/README.md)
- Rule 6 names encryption, masking or tokenisation, access control, access logs kept for a year and backups or continuity as baseline safeguards (see B1). — [dpdpa.com Rule 6](https://dpdpa.com/dpdparules/rule6.html)

### Inferences (engineering recommendations; not individually sourced in this round)
- Encryption at rest: use SQLCipher with a random 256-bit raw key (not a low-entropy passphrase), injected through `fly secrets` as an environment variable at boot and never written to the volume. Fly volume encryption alone does not protect against someone with app or host access.
- Backups: Litestream or periodic `sqlite3 .backup` (or `VACUUM INTO`) produces SQLCipher-encrypted files. Also encrypt with age or restic before uploading to object storage, with a separate key. Test restores.
- Secrets: bank or AA tokens, LLM API keys and session-signing keys go in Fly secrets. Encrypt sensitive columns (for example tokens) at the application level with a key held separately from the database key.
- Tokens and auth (web): HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Strict session cookies rather than localStorage JWTs. Argon2id password hashing. Rate limiting and lockout. Passkeys (WebAuthn) or TOTP for two-factor. CSRF protection. Short sessions with re-authentication before sensitive actions (export, delete, changing the partner link).
- CSP: `default-src 'self'`, no `unsafe-inline` scripts, `frame-ancestors 'none'`, plus HSTS. Render LLM output as text, not HTML, to block injected markup.
- Audit log: an append-only table of who viewed, exported or changed which records, especially partner views of private data. Keep it at least one year to match Rule 6.
- OWASP ASVS chapters on authentication, session management, access control, data protection, logging and secure communication are the relevant checklist. MASVS applies only if a native mobile app ships.

### Gaps
- OWASP ASVS 5.0 and MASVS item numbers were not retrieved this round.
- Fly.io volume encryption specifics and Litestream-with-SQLCipher compatibility were not verified.

## B3. Sending transaction data to LLM APIs: minimisation, pseudonymisation, retention, local models, prompt injection, deterministic numbers

### Takeaway
Default API retention is not zero. Anthropic deletes API inputs and outputs within 30 days, longer if flagged, and zero data retention requires a separate agreement. OpenAI also gates ZDR behind approval. Reports of 2026 changes to both are secondary and partly contradictory. Transaction narrations are untrusted text and a classic indirect prompt-injection vector (OWASP LLM01). The safe design is: code computes every number, the LLM only labels or explains, it gets minimised and pseudonymised input, and it has no tools that can act.

### Cited Findings
- Anthropic commercial API: inputs and outputs are deleted automatically within 30 days. Flagged content may be kept up to 2 years (classifier scores up to 7 years), and feedback for 5 years. ZDR is available only by agreement. — [Anthropic Privacy Center](https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/7996866-how-long-do-you-store-my-organization-s-data)
- Secondary reports (June-August 2026) say Anthropic now requires 30-day retention for its most capable models, while ZDR remains for others. They also report OpenAI announcing expanded ZDR on 19 August 2026. These sources conflict and are unverified, so check vendor docs at build time. — [digitalapplied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/fable-5-30-day-data-retention-zdr-enterprise-2026); [theaicareerlab](https://theaicareerlab.com/blog/anthropic-enterprise-data-retention-policy-2026); [betanews](https://betanews.com/article/openai-private-safety-processing/)
- OpenAI API data is not used for training by default (since 2023), and ZDR on eligible endpoints requires prior approval. — [ConductAtlas summary of OpenAI policy](https://conductatlas.com/platform/openai/openai-api-data-usage-policies/provision/CA-P-061213/zero-data-retention-available-for-eligible-endpoints/) (secondary)
- OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications 2025 has LLM01 Prompt Injection (direct, and indirect through content the model reads) and LLM06 Excessive Agency (too much functionality, permission or autonomy). Injection becomes harmful when combined with agency. Mitigations are to treat model output as untrusted, validate before acting, sandbox, and limit tools. — [promptfoo OWASP mapping](https://promptfoo.dev/docs/red-team/owasp-llm-top-10/); [Confident AI](https://www.confident-ai.com/blog/owasp-top-10-2025-for-llm-applications-risks-and-mitigation-techniques); [Aembit](https://aembit.io/blog/owasp-top-10-llm-risks-explained/) (notes a 2026 edition exists)
- Rule 6 names masking, obfuscation and virtual tokens as safeguards, which supports pseudonymising data before it goes to a processor. — [dpdpa.com Rule 6](https://dpdpa.com/dpdparules/rule6.html)

### Inferences (design recommendations)
- Minimisation: send only what the task needs. For categorisation, that is the cleaned merchant string and maybe the amount bucket, not account numbers, UPI VPAs, names of counterparties or balances. Strip UPI reference IDs, phone numbers, VPAs (`name@okhdfc`) and account fragments with regex before any LLM call.
- Pseudonymisation: replace people and VPAs with stable tokens (P1, P2) and amounts with buckets or relative values. Map them back locally. Merchant brands (Swiggy, Zomato) are low-risk and useful for categorisation, so keep those.
- Deterministic numbers: all totals, rollovers, settle-up balances and projections are computed in Rust. The LLM receives the precomputed figures and only writes prose, or returns a category label from a fixed list (validated against an enum, with the response schema enforced). Never parse numbers out of LLM text into the ledger.
- Prompt injection: UPI and NEFT narrations are attacker-controllable. Anyone who sends ₹1 can put text in your statement. Wrap them as quoted data, give the model no tools or write access (avoiding LLM06), validate outputs against an allowlist, and never let LLM output trigger actions or render as HTML.
- Local versus API: running a small model locally (Ollama or llama.cpp with a 3-8B model) for categorisation keeps all data on the box. On a small Fly VM without a GPU this is slow or memory-bound. A practical split is a local or rules-based categoriser first, and an API model only for occasional narrative "explain my month" summaries over aggregated, pseudonymised figures, with ZDR where available.
- Settings: a per-user toggle for LLM features, off by default for the partner until they opt in. Log every outbound LLM payload in the audit log so the user can see exactly what was sent.

### Gaps
- Official OpenAI and Google Gemini retention pages were not fetched. Their current 2026 terms are unverified.
- No benchmarks were found for local-model transaction categorisation accuracy on Indian bank narrations.
- No documented real-world case of prompt injection through bank narrations was found. The risk is inferred from OWASP LLM01.
